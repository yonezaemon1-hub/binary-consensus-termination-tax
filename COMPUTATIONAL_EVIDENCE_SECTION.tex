\section{Computational evidence}\label{sec:computational-evidence}

The proofs above are analytic.  As an independent finite-parameter audit, the accompanying standard-library script \texttt{audit/computational\_evidence.py} evaluates the stated clockless and periodic-phase bounds on a grid of small and moderate parameter values.  The computation is not used as proof; its purpose is to expose boundary behavior, monotonicity in the free phase period $P$, and possible off-by-one errors in the displayed formulas.

For example, Table~\ref{tab:state-phase-audit} shows representative known-$D$ values from the periodic-phase theorem family.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{c|ccccc}
$D=8$ & $P=1$ & $P=2$ & $P=4$ & $P=8$ & $P=16$ \\
\hline
lower states & 5 & 3 & 3 & 3 & 3 \\
upper states & 10 & 6 & 4 & 3 & 3
\end{tabular}
\caption{Finite arithmetic audit of the published state--phase bounds for $D=8$.  Increasing the externally supplied period reduces the persistent-state requirement, and the constructive upper bound reaches three states at $P=D$.}
\label{tab:state-phase-audit}
\end{table}

The script also exports CSV tables for the clockless known-size/known-diameter bounds and for the periodic-phase known-size/known-diameter bounds.  These finite computations support reproducibility and presentation only; the universal claims remain those proved in the corresponding theorem statements.
